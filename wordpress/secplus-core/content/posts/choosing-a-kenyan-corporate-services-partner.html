If you are dealing with this issue, begin by understanding the applicable requirements and what information you will need. The correct approach depends on your entity and circumstances.

When you, or a client, need support with a Kenyan entity, the local partner you choose matters. These are the questions worth asking.

Credentials

  • Who will oversee the matter, and what are their professional qualifications?
  • Are they registered with the relevant professional body for Certified Secretaries in Kenya?
  • Do they have experience with matters like yours?

Process transparency

  • Will you receive a written scope, deliverables and fees before work starts?
  • Is it clear what the provider does and what other advisers handle?
  • How and how often will progress be reported?

Secure documentation

Corporate work involves identity documents and sensitive records. Ask how documents are transmitted and stored, who has access to them and how the provider meets its obligations under Kenya’s Data Protection Act, 2019.

Service scope

Corporate secretarial providers handle registration, filings and administration. Matters such as litigation, tax planning or immigration may require advocates or other specialists. A good partner is clear about these boundaries and coordinates where agreed.

Communication

Agree channels, time zones, response expectations and points of contact at the start. Clear communication is often the difference between a smooth cross-border engagement and a frustrating one.

Official sources to check

  • The register of the professional body for Certified Secretaries in Kenya
  • Office of the Data Protection Commissioner
  • Business Registration Service (BRS)

This guide provides general information about Kenyan requirements as understood at the date of its last review. It is not legal advice. Requirements and fees change, so confirm the current position with the official sources above or speak to a qualified adviser about your circumstances.
