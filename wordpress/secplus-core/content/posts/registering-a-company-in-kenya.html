Company registration in Kenya is administered by the Registrar of Companies through the Business Registration Service (BRS), with applications made online. Before you begin, it helps to understand the structures available and what happens after registration.

Entity options

  • Business name: registers the name under which a sole proprietor or partnership trades. It does not create a separate legal entity.
  • Private company limited by shares: the most common structure for owner-managed and investor-backed businesses. The company is a separate legal person and shareholders’ liability is limited.
  • Public company: suited to larger organizations that may offer shares to the public, with additional governance requirements.
  • Company limited by guarantee: often used by non-profit, membership and charitable organizations.
  • Foreign company (branch): registration of an overseas company that establishes a place of business in Kenya.

Limited liability partnerships are also available under separate legislation and may suit some professional firms.

Typical steps for a private company

  1. Choose a structure that fits your ownership, liability and funding plans.
  2. Search for and reserve a proposed company name.
  3. Prepare the company’s constitution and the details of directors, shareholders and any secretary.
  4. Provide identification and tax registration details for officers and shareholders, and the registered office address.
  5. Submit the application and pay the prescribed fees.
  6. Receive the certificate of incorporation and set up the company’s statutory records.

Information you will need

  • Proposed company names, in order of preference
  • The nature of the business
  • Names, contact details and identification for each director and shareholder
  • Tax registration (KRA PIN) details where applicable
  • The proposed share capital and how shares will be held
  • A physical registered office address in Kenya
  • Information about beneficial owners: the individuals who ultimately own or control the company

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a structure without considering future investors or exit plans
  • Incomplete or inaccurate beneficial ownership information
  • Treating registration as the finish line and overlooking continuing obligations
  • Not setting up statutory registers and minute books from the start

What happens next

Once registered, a company has continuing obligations, including filing annual returns, maintaining statutory registers, keeping beneficial ownership information up to date and notifying the Registrar of changes. Tax registration, county business permits and sector licences may also apply. Planning for these from day one makes compliance much simpler.

Official sources to check

  • Business Registration Service (BRS)
  • Kenya Law (Companies Act, 2015 and related regulations)
  • Kenya Revenue Authority
