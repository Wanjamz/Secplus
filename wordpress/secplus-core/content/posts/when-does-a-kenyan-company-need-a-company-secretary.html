If you are dealing with this issue, begin by understanding the applicable requirements and what information you will need. The correct approach depends on your entity and circumstances.

Requirements by entity type

Under the Companies Act, 2015, a public company must have a company secretary who holds the professional qualification prescribed by law; in practice, a Certified Public Secretary. A private company must also appoint a secretary once its paid-up capital reaches the threshold set in the Act.

Other private companies are not always required to appoint a secretary, but many do, because the role keeps statutory records and procedures in order. Check the current thresholds against the Act before relying on them.

What the role involves

  • Maintaining statutory registers and the company’s minute books
  • Preparing and filing annual returns and change notifications
  • Organizing board and shareholder meetings, notices and minutes
  • Preparing resolutions and keeping records of decisions
  • Advising directors on procedure and corporate governance
  • Keeping a calendar of statutory deadlines

Appointment

The directors usually appoint the company secretary. The appointment is recorded in the company’s register of secretaries and notified to the Registrar. The secretary can be an individual or, in some cases, a firm.

Ongoing responsibilities

The board remains responsible for the company’s decisions and compliance. A company secretary, whether in-house or outsourced, supports the board by making sure procedures are followed and records are kept accurately.

Official sources to check

  • Kenya Law (Companies Act, 2015 and related regulations)
  • The register of the professional body for Certified Secretaries in Kenya
  • Business Registration Service (BRS)

This guide provides general information about Kenyan requirements as understood at the date of its last review. It is not legal advice. Requirements and fees change, so confirm the current position with the official sources above or speak to a qualified adviser about your circumstances.
