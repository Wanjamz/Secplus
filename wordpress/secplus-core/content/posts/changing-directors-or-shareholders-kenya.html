If you are dealing with this issue, begin by understanding the applicable requirements and what information you will need. The correct approach depends on your entity and circumstances.

Changes in directors and shareholders are among the most common corporate events. Handling them properly keeps your records accurate and avoids problems in later due diligence.

Decision records

Start with your company’s constitution and any shareholders’ agreement. They set out who can appoint or remove directors and any restrictions on transferring shares, such as pre-emption rights. Record the decision in a board or shareholder resolution, as required.

Documentation

  • New director: consent to act, identification and contact details, and tax registration details where required.
  • Director leaving: resignation letter or the resolution removing the director, following the required procedure.
  • Share transfer: a share transfer instrument, any required approvals, and evidence that stamp duty has been assessed and paid where applicable.
  • New shares: the resolutions authorizing the allotment and the return of allotment.

Filings and registers

  • Update the register of directors or register of members
  • Notify the Registrar of the change within the period required by law
  • Update beneficial ownership information if ownership or control has changed
  • Issue new share certificates where shares have changed hands

Advisory considerations

Some changes have wider implications. Transfers may have tax consequences, a change of control can trigger approvals from sector regulators or competition authorities, and bank mandates and licences may need updating. Identify these early.

Official sources to check

  • Business Registration Service (BRS)
  • Kenya Law (Companies Act, 2015 and related regulations)
  • Kenya Revenue Authority

This guide provides general information about Kenyan requirements as understood at the date of its last review. It is not legal advice. Requirements and fees change, so confirm the current position with the official sources above or speak to a qualified adviser about your circumstances.
