If you are dealing with this issue, begin by understanding the applicable requirements and what information you will need. The correct approach depends on your entity and circumstances.

What is an annual return?

An annual return is a filing with the Registrar of Companies that confirms key information about a company as at its return date, usually linked to the anniversary of incorporation. It provides the public record with up-to-date information about the company.

Who is responsible?

The company’s directors are responsible for ensuring the annual return is filed. In practice, the company secretary or an external corporate services provider usually prepares and files it.

Records the return relies on

  • Registered office address
  • Details of directors and any company secretary
  • Share capital and shareholdings
  • Details of members (shareholders)
  • Any changes that should already have been notified to the Registrar

If your registers are out of date, the annual return is a good prompt to bring them in order.

Deadlines

The Companies Act, 2015 requires the annual return to be delivered within a short period after the company’s return date. Confirm your company’s return date and the current filing deadline on the BRS portal, and diarize it each year. Late filing can attract penalties.

Correcting overdue returns

  1. Review the company’s registers and filing history to identify what is outstanding.
  2. Bring registers up to date and file any outstanding change notifications.
  3. Prepare and file the outstanding annual returns.
  4. Pay any penalties that apply.
  5. Put a compliance calendar in place to avoid a repeat.

Official sources to check

  • Business Registration Service (BRS)
  • Kenya Law (Companies Act, 2015 and related regulations)
