Many Kenyan founders start by asking whether to register a business name or incorporate a limited company. Both are registered through the Business Registration Service, but they create very different legal positions.

What is a business name registration?

A business name registration records the name under which an individual (sole proprietor) or a partnership trades. The business is not a separate legal entity: the owners are the business, and they are personally responsible for its debts and obligations.

What is a limited company?

A private limited company is incorporated under the Companies Act, 2015. It is a separate legal person that can own property, enter contracts and be sued in its own name. Shareholders’ liability is generally limited to any amount unpaid on their shares.

Key differences

  • Liability: personal for business name owners; generally limited for company shareholders.
  • Ownership and investment: companies can issue shares, which makes it easier to bring in co-founders and investors.
  • Governance: companies have directors, shareholders and a constitution, and must follow formal decision-making procedures.
  • Records and filings: companies must keep statutory registers and file annual returns and beneficial ownership information.
  • Perception: some clients, lenders and tenders prefer or require a registered company.

Suitability considerations

  • How much personal risk are you prepared to carry?
  • Do you plan to bring in partners or investors?
  • Will customers or funders expect a company?
  • Are you prepared to maintain company records and filings?
  • How might tax treatment differ for your situation? (Seek tax advice.)

There is no single right answer. The choice depends on your plans and circumstances, and it is possible to move from a business name to a company later. This guide is not individual legal advice.

Official sources to check

  • Business Registration Service (BRS)
  • Kenya Law (Companies Act, 2015 and related regulations)
